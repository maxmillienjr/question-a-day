30 days with a veteran

One question a day for thirty days. Ask it, then the follow-up, and write down what you hear in their words. Let them lead. Skip anything they do not want to go into, and believe them the first time they say so.

  1. Day 1 How did you come to join the service? Where were you living, and what were you doing when it was decided?

    Then: Who did you tell first, and how did they take it?

    Tip: Let them lead. Skip anything they do not want to go into. This question starts before any of the hard parts, on purpose.

  2. Day 2 Tell me about the day you left for training. How did you get there?

    Then: What did you bring with you?

    Tip: Travel stories warm people up. Ask who was sitting next to them.

  3. Day 3 What do you remember about your first few days of training? Pick one moment.

    Then: Who was the first person you got to know there?

    Tip: Training is often where the funny stories live. Let them laugh.

  4. Day 4 Who was in charge of you in training that you still remember? What did they look and sound like?

    Then: What is something they said that stayed with you?

    Tip: Ask for the voice. People often remember exactly how an instructor said something.

  5. Day 5 Once training was over, what did a normal day look like? Take me from waking up to lights out.

    Then: What was the food like?

    Tip: Ordinary days are safer ground than big ones, and they are the part nobody else will remember.

  6. Day 6 Who did you share quarters with? Tell me about one of them.

    Then: Where were they from, and what were they like?

    Tip: Write every name down. Names are threads you can come back to.

  7. Day 7 What did you keep with you, in a pocket or a bag, that you cared about?

    Then: Where is it now?

    Tip: If they still have it, ask them to bring it out. Objects make it easier to talk.

  8. Day 8 Tell me about a letter, call or package from home that you remember.

    Then: Who wrote to you most often?

    Tip: If letters survive in a drawer, ask before reading any, and let them choose which ones.

  9. Day 9 What is a place where you served that you can still picture? Describe it.

    Then: What was the weather like, and what did it smell like?

    Tip: Let them pick the place. They may skip some, and that is their choice.

  10. Day 10 Tell me about one day off that you remember. Where did you go?

    Then: Who were you with?

    Tip: Days off are where the friendships show.

  11. Day 11 What was your job day to day? Show me with your hands if it helps.

    Then: What part of it were you good at?

    Tip: Asking what they were good at puts them in the expert's chair.

  12. Day 12 Who do you still think about from your time in service? Tell me about them.

    Then: What would you say to them if they walked in?

    Tip: This one can bring a long pause. Do not fill it. Count to ten in your head.

  13. Day 13 What is the funniest thing you remember from your time in service?

    Then: Who was in on it?

    Tip: Funny stories are a way in. More serious ones may come later, in their own time, or not at all.

  14. Day 14 Tell me about a person you met in the service who was nothing like the people back home.

    Then: What did you learn from them?

    Tip: People from somewhere else often explain a whole period of someone's life.

  15. Day 15 Tell me about a holiday you spent in the service. Where were you?

    Then: What did people do to make it feel like a holiday?

    Tip: Around any holiday, this is a gentle question for the table.

  16. Day 16 What was the hardest part of being away? Answer as much or as little as you like.

    Then: What helped?

    Tip: A good day to remind them they can skip anything. "That's fine" is all you need to say.

  17. Day 17 What do people who were not there tend to get wrong about it? Where did you first notice?

    Then: What would you want them to understand instead?

    Tip: People who will not talk about themselves will often happily correct a movie.

  18. Day 18 Tell me about the day you found out you were going home, or getting out.

    Then: What did you do first?

    Tip: Let them tell it in order. Do not jump ahead to how they felt.

  19. Day 19 What was coming home like? Tell me about the first day or the first week.

    Then: Who did you see first?

    Tip: Many veterans say nobody ever asked about this part. Take your time with it.

  20. Day 20 What did you do for work after you got out, and how did you find that first job?

    Then: What from the service did you bring to it?

    Tip: This connects the service years to the rest of their life. Ask about the bridge between them.

  21. Day 21 Who from your service did you see again years later? Tell me about that day.

    Then: Who did you lose touch with that you still wonder about?

    Tip: If a call or a letter still seems possible, write the name down.

  22. Day 22 What did you keep from your service: papers, photographs, anything at all? Tell me the story behind one thing.

    Then: Who else has seen it?

    Tip: Ask before handling anything. Let them hold it while they talk.

  23. Day 23 Tell me about a conversation with your family about your service that you remember.

    Then: What did you want them to understand?

    Tip: If you were part of that conversation, let them tell it their way first.

  24. Day 24 What music brings that time back? Where were you when you heard it?

    Then: Who was with you?

    Tip: If you can find the song, play it, then wait.

  25. Day 25 What is a habit from the service you still have? When did you notice you still had it?

    Then: Who teases you about it?

    Tip: Habits are a light way to talk about how the service stayed with them.

  26. Day 26 Tell me about a Veterans Day or Memorial Day you remember from the years after. Where were you?

    Then: Who were you with?

    Tip: Some veterans keep away from these days. If so, ask how they spend them instead.

  27. Day 27 Tell me about a time someone younger asked you about your service. What did you tell them?

    Then: What did you save for another time?

    Tip: If someone younger in the family is willing, let them ask today's questions.

  28. Day 28 Think of your first day in the service. What would you tell yourself on that day?

    Then: Who gave you good advice back then?

    Tip: Write down the advice in their exact words.

  29. Day 29 What are you proudest of from that time? Tell me about the day it happened.

    Then: Who else knows this story?

    Tip: Write it down in their words, not yours.

  30. Day 30 What do you want the family to remember about your service? Tell me the story you would want told.

    Then: What would you rather we kept to ourselves?

    Tip: Read back what you wrote and ask if you got it right. If they would like their story kept for good, the Library of Congress Veterans History Project collects interviews from families.

Several topics follow the outline of the Library of Congress Veterans History Project sample interview questions (https://www.loc.gov/programs/veterans-history-project/how-to-participate/sample-interview-questions/). The wording is ours.
