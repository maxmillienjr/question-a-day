30 days before the holidays

One question a day for thirty days. Ask it, then the follow-up, and write down what you hear in their words.

  1. Day 1 What is a Thanksgiving, or any big family meal, from years ago that you can still picture? Where was it?

    Then: Who was at the table, and who did the cooking?

    Tip: Today everyone who remembers may be in the same room. Ask while the plates are mostly empty and nobody has stood up yet.

  2. Day 2 Who hosted the big holiday meals when you were young, and what was their kitchen like?

    Then: What job were you given once you were old enough to help?

    Tip: Leftovers day. Ask over a plate of them. It is the easiest setting there is.

  3. Day 3 What dish showed up every year, made the same way every time? Who made it?

    Then: Where is that recipe now: on a card, in a book, or only in someone's head?

    Tip: If the recipe lives only in their head, offer to cook it with them this month and write it down as you go.

  4. Day 4 What holidays did the household you grew up in celebrate? Walk me through one of those days from the morning on.

    Then: What was the one rule everyone had to follow that day?

    Tip: Do not assume you know the traditions. Ask, even if you think you know.

  5. Day 5 What is the first winter you remember clearly? Where were you living?

    Then: How did you keep warm, and what did you wear outside?

    Tip: Weather is an easy way into a memory. Cold rooms and snowed-in days come back fast.

  6. Day 6 Tell me about a gift you gave someone that you are still pleased about.

    Then: How did you pay for it, or how did you make it?

    Tip: Asking about a gift they gave, not one they got, takes them somewhere less expected.

  7. Day 7 What is a gift you were given as a child that you remember, even a small one? Who gave it to you?

    Then: Where did you keep it?

    Tip: Gifts were scarce in some homes. If that was true for them, ask what the day felt like instead.

  8. Day 8 What did the house look like at the holidays when you were growing up? Who did the decorating, if anyone?

    Then: What happened to those things?

    Tip: If anything survived, an ornament, a candle holder, a tablecloth, ask them to bring it out.

  9. Day 9 Tell me about a holiday that went wrong. What happened?

    Then: Who still brings it up?

    Tip: Disasters make the best holiday stories, and most of them are safe to laugh about now.

  10. Day 10 Where did people gather around the holidays when you were young: a church, a temple, a hall, a neighbor's house? Describe walking in.

    Then: Who did you always see there?

    Tip: If the answer is nowhere, ask where people gathered instead. For a lot of families it was the kitchen.

  11. Day 11 What songs or music do you connect with this time of year? Where did you hear them?

    Then: Who sang along, and who refused to?

    Tip: Play one of the songs tonight and see what it brings back.

  12. Day 12 Tell me about a holiday you spent away from home. Where were you?

    Then: How did you mark the day there?

    Tip: Holidays away are often the ones people remember best, because they were different.

  13. Day 13 Tell me about the first holiday you hosted or planned yourself. Where was it?

    Then: What did you get wrong, and what did you get right?

    Tip: Ask how it compared with the ones they grew up with. That comparison is a story in itself.

  14. Day 14 Who was the person at every holiday who told the stories? What was one they always told?

    Then: How did they start it?

    Tip: Ask for the story the way that person told it. A second-hand story is still worth keeping.

  15. Day 15 What did you do on the long days off school in winter when you were a kid?

    Then: Who did you spend those days with?

    Tip: Children's games and chores tell you a lot about a place and a time.

  16. Day 16 What is a tradition from your family that faded out? When do you remember it happening last?

    Then: Why do you think it ended?

    Tip: If it is one worth bringing back, this month is a good time to try it once together.

  17. Day 17 What is a holiday you remember because it was different from all the rest? What made it different?

    Then: Who was missing that year, or who was there for the first time?

    Tip: Different years stick and ordinary years blur. Start with the one that stands out.

  18. Day 18 What did you look forward to most in December when you were young, and where did it happen?

    Then: Who made it happen?

    Tip: Ask about the waiting. The anticipation is often remembered better than the day.

  19. Day 19 What smell or taste means winter to you? Where does it take you?

    Then: Who was in that kitchen?

    Tip: If you can make it this week, make it, and ask the question again while it is cooking.

  20. Day 20 Tell me about the most crowded holiday you remember. Where did everyone sleep?

    Then: Who snored, who argued and who got up first?

    Tip: Questions about logistics get people laughing. Sleeping arrangements are a classic.

  21. Day 21 What is a letter or card you got around the holidays that you remember? Who was it from?

    Then: What did it say, as near as you can remember?

    Tip: If there is a box of old cards somewhere, this is the week to open it together.

  22. Day 22 What did your street or town look like at night this time of year when you were growing up?

    Then: Where did you walk, and who walked with you?

    Tip: Ask what they could see from the window. Small details carry a whole place.

  23. Day 23 Who is someone you used to spend the holidays with who is not here anymore? Tell me about one holiday with them.

    Then: What would they be doing right now if they were in the room?

    Tip: This one can bring tears, and that is all right. Tears are not a reason to stop unless they say so.

  24. Day 24 Tell me about a holiday in your twenties. Where were you living, and who were you with?

    Then: What did you think your life would look like by now?

    Tip: Asking what they expected back then is a gentle way into how things turned out.

  25. Day 25 What is the best New Year's Eve you remember? Where were you?

    Then: What were you hoping for that year?

    Tip: Some people never cared for New Year's. If so, ask what they did instead.

  26. Day 26 Tell me about a time someone outside the family was kind to you around the holidays. What happened?

    Then: What would you say to them now?

    Tip: Kindness stories are good ones to share with the whole table.

  27. Day 27 Who did you write to or call every year at this time? Tell me about them.

    Then: Where did they live, and what did you talk about?

    Tip: A name from an old card list is a thread worth pulling.

  28. Day 28 What did children do the night before a big holiday where you grew up? What did you do?

    Then: How did you try to stay awake, or how were you made to go to sleep?

    Tip: If grandchildren are around, let one of them ask this one.

  29. Day 29 Tell me about a holiday eve you remember clearly, whatever your family celebrated. Where were you?

    Then: What did you eat, and who was there?

    Tip: If your family celebrates tonight, ask between courses. If not, a quiet evening is the best time of all.

  30. Day 30 Which holiday tradition do you most want the family to keep? Tell me about the first time you remember it.

    Then: Who started it?

    Tip: Write their answer down word for word and read it back at next year's table.
