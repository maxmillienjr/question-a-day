30 days with Mom or Dad

One question a day for thirty days. Ask it, then the follow-up, and write down what you hear in their words.

  1. Day 1 What is the first house or apartment you remember living in? Walk me through the front door.

    Then: Which room did you spend the most time in, and why that one?

    Tip: Start with a place. Places are easier to describe than feelings, and the people show up on their own.

  2. Day 2 Who lived on your street when you were small? Pick one neighbor you can still picture.

    Then: What did that person do that you noticed as a kid?

    Tip: If they name someone you have never heard of, write the name down. It is a thread for another day.

  3. Day 3 What did a school day look like for you at about ten years old, from the time you got up?

    Then: How did you get to school, and who went with you?

    Tip: Ask for the ordinary day, not the big one. The ordinary day is the one nobody wrote down.

  4. Day 4 Which teacher or grown-up outside the family do you still think about? Tell me about one day with them.

    Then: What is one thing they said that stuck with you?

    Tip: Let them take their time. A pause usually means they are finding the picture.

  5. Day 5 What was an ordinary weeknight supper where you grew up, and who made it?

    Then: Which dish from back then could you still make, or still taste?

    Tip: Food questions work at a busy table. Everyone has an opinion, and the answers are funny more often than sad.

  6. Day 6 What was your first paying job? Take me to the first day.

    Then: What did you do with the first money you earned?

    Tip: Ask what the work smelled and sounded like. The senses bring back the details.

  7. Day 7 Find a photograph of yourself from when you were young. What was happening just outside the frame?

    Then: Who took it, and what happened right after?

    Tip: A person who cannot start a story can almost always explain a photo. Let the picture do some of the asking.

  8. Day 8 Who was your closest friend when you were a teenager? Tell me about one afternoon you spent together.

    Then: Where did the two of you go when you wanted to get away from everyone?

    Tip: If the friend has died, give it a moment before the next question. Then ask what they were like.

  9. Day 9 What song takes you straight back to a particular day when you were young? Where were you?

    Then: Who were you with when you heard it?

    Tip: If you can, play the song for them. Then be quiet and let it work.

  10. Day 10 What was the first place you lived on your own, or away from the people who raised you?

    Then: What did it cost, and what did you put in it?

    Tip: Money details are gold for a story. Ask for prices, not just places.

  11. Day 11 Tell me about a trip you still think about. Where did you go, and who was with you?

    Then: What went wrong on that trip?

    Tip: Asking what went wrong usually gets a better story than asking what was best.

  12. Day 12 What is a skill someone taught you by hand? Who taught you, and where were you?

    Then: What did they do differently from how people do it now?

    Tip: If it is something they can show you, ask them to show you. Hands remember.

  13. Day 13 Tell me about a holiday from when you were young that you remember clearly, good or not. Where was it?

    Then: Who was there, and who sat where?

    Tip: Some holidays were hard. If the answer turns heavy, follow it gently or move on. Both are fine.

  14. Day 14 Where were you when you heard a piece of big news, the kind everyone remembers?

    Then: Who was the first person you talked to about it?

    Tip: News days are easy to place in time, which helps you put the rest of the story in order later.

  15. Day 15 What is a job or a role you were proud of, paid or not? Tell me about one day of it.

    Then: Who did you work alongside, and what were they like?

    Tip: Halfway. Read back one answer from the first two weeks and ask if you got it right. The corrections are often the best part.

  16. Day 16 What is the hardest physical work you have ever done? Where were you?

    Then: How did you feel at the end of that day?

    Tip: When a line lands, write down their exact words. Your summary will never be as good as how they said it.

  17. Day 17 Tell me about a time you got lost, stuck or stranded somewhere. How did you get home?

    Then: Who helped, or who should have?

    Tip: Small emergencies make good stories, and they are usually safe to tell at the table.

  18. Day 18 Who made you laugh the most when you were growing up? Tell me about one time.

    Then: What would they say if they walked in right now?

    Tip: Ask for an impression. People will often do a voice they have not done in years.

  19. Day 19 What drive, bus ride or train route do you remember best? Where did it take you?

    Then: What did you see out the window?

    Tip: A trip gives a story a beginning, a middle and an end without anyone trying.

  20. Day 20 Tell me about a decision that changed where your life went. Where were you when you made it?

    Then: Who did you tell first?

    Tip: Do not ask whether it was the right call. Ask what it was like to make it.

  21. Day 21 Where did you go to be alone, or to think, when you were younger? Describe it.

    Then: What did you think about there?

    Tip: If they say nowhere, ask where they went when the house got loud.

  22. Day 22 Tell me about someone you lost touch with that you still wonder about.

    Then: Where did you last see them?

    Tip: Write down every name you hear today. A list of names is worth a second conversation.

  23. Day 23 What did a Saturday look like for you in your twenties or thirties? Pick one you remember.

    Then: Where did the money go back then, and where did the time go?

    Tip: Decades are easier to walk through than single years. Let them pick the decade.

  24. Day 24 What is something you made, built, grew or fixed that you are proud of? Tell me about making it.

    Then: Where is it now?

    Tip: Pride is an easier door than regret. Go in through it first.

  25. Day 25 Tell me about a meal you will never forget. Where were you, and who was at the table?

    Then: What was said at that table that you still remember?

    Tip: At a family meal, ask while the plates are mostly empty and nobody has stood up yet.

  26. Day 26 Of the people who raised you, who do you think about most? Tell me about one ordinary day with them.

    Then: What do you wish you had asked them?

    Tip: This question often turns the conversation around. Let it.

  27. Day 27 What is a piece of advice you ignored? Where were you when you heard it?

    Then: What happened instead?

    Tip: Laughter is allowed. Most of these answers are funnier than you expect.

  28. Day 28 Tell me about a year that was hard, and one small thing that got you through it.

    Then: Who showed up for you that year?

    Tip: Only if they want to. "I don't want to get into that" is a full answer. Say "that's fine" and ask about the small thing instead.

  29. Day 29 If you could go back to one place from your life tomorrow, where would it be? What would you do first when you got there?

    Then: Who would you want with you?

    Tip: If a small version of this trip is possible, even a drive past an old address, write that down too.

  30. Day 30 What is one moment from your life you would want people to know about a hundred years from now? Tell it the way you want it told.

    Then: Why that one?

    Tip: Write this answer down word for word and read it back to them. Then thank them for the thirty days.
