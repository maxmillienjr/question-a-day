A Question a Day, in Your Calendar

Add one of these calendars and, for the next 30 days, each day's date carries one question to ask your mom, your dad or a grandparent, with a follow-up and a short tip. On day 30 it stops.

No sign-up. No email address. Nothing to unsubscribe from: remove the calendar and it is gone. We made it because the hardest part of getting a parent's story down is asking the first question, and the second one the next day.

What you will see

Each question is an all-day item at the top of its day, the way a holiday or a birthday shows up. Open it for the follow-up and the tip.

Whether your phone also sends you a reminder depends on the app, and we cannot promise one. Each day carries a 9 a.m. reminder, but some calendar apps drop reminders from calendars added by link. How to get a reminder you can count on.

How to add it

iPhone or iPad

Tap Apple Calendar on this phone and confirm. Or copy the link, then in the Calendar app tap Calendars, Add Calendar, Add Subscription Calendar, and paste it. If you pick iCloud as the account, it shows on your other Apple devices too.

Mac

Click Apple Calendar, or in Calendar choose File, New Calendar Subscription and paste the link. In the window that follows, untick Remove alerts if you want the 9 a.m. reminder.

Google Calendar, including Android phones

This step needs a computer. Google's own help says calendars cannot be added by link in the Google Calendar app on Android, iPhone or iPad. Click Google Calendar on a computer signed in to your Google account (or in Google Calendar, choose Other calendars, the plus sign, From URL, and paste the link). Once it is added, it shows up in the app on your phone by itself.

Outlook

Click Outlook to open Outlook on the web, or in Outlook choose Add calendar, Subscribe from web, paste the link and choose Import. Work or school accounts can do the same at outlook.office.com. If the new Outlook for Windows does not add it, add it on the web instead. Microsoft's help does not describe adding a calendar by link in the Outlook phone app; add it on the web and it appears there.

A reminder you can count on

Calendars added by link are read-only, and apps treat their reminders differently. Apple lets you turn alerts on for a subscribed calendar (on iPhone, tap Calendars, then the i beside it). Google and Microsoft do not say whether reminders from a calendar added by link will reach you.

If a daily nudge matters to you, use Download instead and import the file into your own calendar. The days then behave like any event you made yourself, and you set reminders the way you always do. Tip: in Google Calendar or Apple Calendar, make a new calendar first and import into that, so you can remove all 30 days at once later.

Started late?

A calendar added by link has fixed dates. "30 days with Mom or Dad" and "30 days with a veteran" start the day after you add them. The holiday one runs from Thanksgiving to December 25, 2026, so if you add it partway through, the earlier days are already behind you; its printable version has all thirty. Its questions are written for those dates, so its download keeps them too. For the other two, the download lets you start on any date you like.
