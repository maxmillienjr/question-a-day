"Sledgehammer" was the first Hot 100 number one for Peter Gabriel. Genesis, the band which he had fronted in the 1970s, also reached the top spot for the first time in 1986.
The Billboard Hot 100 is a chart published since August 1958 by Billboard magazine which ranks the best-performing singles in the United States. In 1986, it was compiled based on a combination of sales and airplay data sourced from surveys of retail outlets and playlists submitted by radio stations, respectively. During the year, 31 different singles spent time at number one.
At the start of the year, "Say You, Say Me" by Lionel Richie held the number-one position, having reached the top spot in the issue of Billboard dated December 21. It was replaced in the issue dated January 18 by "That's What Friends Are For", credited to Dionne & Friends. A charity single intended to raise funds for HIV/AIDS-related causes, the song was performed by Dionne Warwick, Elton John, Gladys Knight and Stevie Wonder. It was the first Hot 100 number one for Knight without her long-time backing group the Pips. In March, the rock band Heart, which had charted regularly on the Hot 100 since 1976, reached number one for the first time with "These Dreams". In contrast, the song which replaced it at number one, "Rock Me Amadeus", was the first single by the Austrian musician Falco to enter the Hot 100.
In July, the British rock band Genesis reached number one with "Invisible Touch"; it was the first Hot 100 chart-topper for the group, which had been active since the late 1960s and had first charted in the United States in 1977. After a single week in the top spot, the single was displaced by "Sledgehammer", the first number one for Peter Gabriel, who had been the lead singer of Genesis from its formation until 1975.
Robert Palmer, Pet Shop Boys, Simply Red, Steve Winwood, Bananarama, Berlin, Janet Jackson, Boston, Bon Jovi, Amy Grant, Bruce Hornsby & the Range, and the Bangles also topped the Hot 100 for the first time. Patti LaBelle, Michael McDonald, and Peter Cetera, already having hit number one with Labelle, the Doobie Brothers, and Chicago, respectively, also earned their first number one songs as solo acts. Whitney Houston, Madonna, and Cetera were the only acts to earn more than one number one song, with each of them hitting the top twice.
Chart history
Whitney Houston had two number ones in 1986.
Peter Cetera (pictured in 2017) had two chart-toppers.
The Bangles gained their first number one.
"When I Think of You" was the first number one for Janet Jackson (pictured in 1998).
