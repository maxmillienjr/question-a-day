Dexys Midnight Runners were one of a number of British acts with Hot 100 number ones in 1983.
The Billboard Hot 100 is a chart published since August 1958 by Billboard magazine which ranks the best-performing singles in the United States. In 1983, it was compiled based on a combination of sales and airplay data sourced from surveys of retail outlets and playlists submitted by radio stations, respectively. During the year, 17 different singles spent time at number one.
At the start of the year, Daryl Hall & John Oates held the number-one position with "Maneater". It was displaced from the top spot by "Down Under" by Men at Work in the issue of Billboard dated January 15. The latter song spent four non-consecutive weeks atop the Hot 100, the run being interrupted for a single week by "Africa", the debut number one for the band Toto. In February, "Baby, Come to Me" by Patti Austin and James Ingram reached number one, the first chart-topper for either singer. The duet had originally been released early in the previous year and achieved little success; after it was featured on the television soap opera General Hospital, sparking renewed interest in the song, it was re-released and went to number one. After two weeks atop the Hot 100, it was replaced by "Billie Jean", the first of three chart-toppers achieved in 1983 by Michael Jackson. No other act took more than one single to number one during the year. "Billie Jean" spent seven weeks at number one; after one week out of the top spot, Jackson returned to the top of the Hot 100 with "Beat It". Both singles were taken from his album Thriller, one of the best-selling and most critically-acclaimed records of all time.
Between Jackson's first two chart-toppers of 1983, Dexys Midnight Runners spent a single week at number one with "Come On Eileen". It was the only top 40 entry for the British band, which is considered a one-hit wonder in the United States. The band was associated with the so-called Second British Invasion of the United States music scene, which led to British acts dominating the upper reaches of the Hot 100 during 1983. Another British group, the Police, reached number one in the United States for the first time with "Every Breath You Take", which spent eight weeks in the peak position, the year's longest run atop the Hot 100. In one week of its run at number one, seven of the singles in the top ten were by acts from the United Kingdom. Two other British acts, Eurythmics and Bonnie Tyler, gained their first Hot 100 chart-toppers in 1983; Eurythmics had never charted on the Hot 100 before and Tyler's only previous entry had been in 1978. Irene Cara reached the top of the Hot 100 for the first and only time with "Flashdance... What a Feeling" in May and Michael Sembello did the same in September with "Maniac"; both songs were taken from the soundtrack of the film Flashdance. "Flashdance... What a Feeling" won the Academy Award for Best Original Song at the following April's ceremony. The final chart-topper of 1983 was "Say Say Say", a collaboration between Paul McCartney and Michael Jackson. The third number one of the year for Jackson, it reached the top spot on the chart dated December 10 and remained there for the rest of the year.
Chart history
Michael Jackson had the year's most Hot 100 number ones of any act and spent the most weeks in the top spot.
"Total Eclipse of the Heart" was a chart-topper for the Welsh singer Bonnie Tyler (pictured in 2013).
Patti Austin and James Ingram (pictured in 2000 and 1998 respectively) topped the chart with their duet "Baby, Come to Me".
David Bowie topped the chart with "Let's Dance".
