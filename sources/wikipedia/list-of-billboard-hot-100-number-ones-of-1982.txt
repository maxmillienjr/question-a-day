Stevie Wonder and Paul McCartney spent seven weeks at number one with "Ebony and Ivory".
The Billboard Hot 100 is a chart published since August 1958 by Billboard magazine which ranks the best-performing singles in the United States. In 1982, it was compiled based on a combination of sales and airplay data sourced from surveys of retail outlets and playlists submitted by radio stations, respectively. During the year, 16 different singles spent time at number one.
At the start of the year, "Physical" by Olivia Newton-John was at number one. The song had held the top spot for the final six weeks of 1981 and added a further four weeks in 1982 for a final total of ten weeks at number one, tying the record set by Debby Boone's "You Light Up My Life" in 1977 for the longest-running chart-topper in the history of the Hot 100. In the issue of Billboard dated January 30, "Physical" was displaced from number one by "I Can't Go for That (No Can Do)" by Daryl Hall & John Oates. The duo returned to number one in December with "Maneater" and was the only act with multiple chart-toppers in 1982. The two longest running number-one singles of 1982 were "I Love Rock 'n' Roll" by Joan Jett and the Blackhearts and "Ebony and Ivory" by Paul McCartney and Stevie Wonder, which each stayed at the top for seven weeks. Additionally, the J. Geils Band and Survivor had six-week runs at number one with "Centerfold" and "Eye of the Tiger" respectively; these long runs contributed to the Hot 100 having the fewest number ones in a calendar year since 1968.
Although McCartney and Wonder had each had many number ones prior to their collaboration, none of the other acts that spent six or more weeks atop the Hot 100 in 1982 had reached the top spot before. "Centerfold" was the first number one for the J. Geils Band, more than a decade after the group first entered the Hot 100. In contrast, "I Love Rock 'n' Roll" was the first single by Joan Jett and the Blackhearts to chart. "Eye of the Tiger", taken from the soundtrack of the film Rocky III, was the first number one for Survivor. Another film theme, "Chariots of Fire", taken from the film of the same name, gave Vangelis a number one with the only Hot 100 entry of his career. In July, the Human League topped the Hot 100 for the first time with "Don't You Want Me", which was later named by Rolling Stone as the "breakthrough song" of the Second British Invasion of the United States music scene, which led to British acts dominating the upper reaches of the Hot 100 in 1983. In October, John Cougar gained his first chart-topper with "Jack & Diane"; the singer appended his real surname to his stage name shortly after this breakthrough to become John Cougar Mellencamp and since the start of the 1990s has recorded as simply John Mellencamp. Cougar's song was replaced at number one by "Who Can It Be Now?", the first number one and first charting single by the Australian band Men at Work. The year's third film theme to top the chart was "Up Where We Belong" by Joe Cocker and Jennifer Warnes, from An Officer and a Gentleman. Neither singer had achieved a previous number one, although Cocker had first charted as far back as 1968. Toni Basil was 1982's final act to gain a career first number one when "Mickey" spent a single week in the top spot in December.
Chart history
Olivia Newton-John (pictured in 2012) began the year at number one with "Physical", continuing a run which ultimately lasted ten weeks.
Joan Jett and the Blackhearts (Jett pictured) tied for the year's longest-running chart-topper.
"Jack & Diane" was a chart-topper for John Cougar. Since the early 1990s, the singer (pictured in 2007) has recorded under his real name, John Mellencamp.
Lionel Richie spent two weeks at number one with "Truly".
